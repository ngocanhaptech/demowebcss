Understanding agentic AI starts with defining AI agents.
At their core, they're autonomous systems that make decisions and take action to achieve
goals.
Proactive problem solvers built to navigate complex environments.
AI agents perform multi-step reasoning, breaking down complex tasks into manageable steps.
They also engage in decision-making, helping choose the best course of action.
AI agents can call external tools, APIs, or even other agents to extend their capabilities.
They can also retain context from past interactions.
Finally, their goal-oriented behavior ensures that decisions and actions achieve a defined
objective.
Agentic AI represents a shift from reactive to proactive systems.
Think of the difference between a calculator and a research assistant.
A calculator is reactive and responds only to inputs.
Agentic AI is like a research assistant who understands your goal, breaks it into steps,
uses tools to gather information, and keeps working until the problem is solved.
You might wonder, why not build agents from scratch?
These frameworks solve infrastructure problems such as memory, tool integration, and agent
coordination so you can focus on your specific problem.
Think of them as the rails of AI development.
Before exploring frameworks, let's understand multi-agent systems.
Instead of one AI doing everything, you have multiple agents working together, such as
a software team with front-end developers, back-end developers, and testers, each contributing
their expertise to achieve better results than anyone could alone.
The key benefits of using multi-agent systems include specialization, where each agent is
designed to excel at a specific task.
Next is parallel processing, where multiple agents work simultaneously.
Then comes fault tolerance, where the system continues to function smoothly.
Next is scalability, where more agents are added as complexity grows.
And finally, modularity, where it's easy to update or replace individual components.
Building agentic systems from scratch is far more complex than working with single agents,
and without frameworks, you're stuck managing complex message protocols, manually syncing
state across agents, and writing custom coordination logic.
You also need to handle errors across agents and monitoring or debugging challenges.
Frameworks simplify all of this.
Communication protocols are built in.
There's automatic state management.
You can use predefined coordination patterns instead of writing your own, while error handling
is standardized and distributed.
Multi-agent tools make it easier to monitor and debug your agentic system.
This video introduces various agentic frameworks, such as Crew AI, Langraph, Autogen, AG2, Pydantic
AI, and BAI.
Let's get started.
Crew AI shines at simulating multi-agent collaboration.
You define agents with distinct roles, such as researcher, writer, and editor, each assigned
specific tasks.
Together they work as a coordinated crew to accomplish complex goals, such as writing
a blog.
Crew AI makes it easy for agents to collaborate by assigning clear roles and tasks.
Structured outputs simplify collaboration among agents.
Langraph lets you control workflows using a directed graph, where each node represents
a step, such as calling a tool, prompting an LLM, or making a decision.
And each edge defines what happens next, depending on the outcome.
This structure gives you fine-grained control.
You define how information flows by designing interactions between nodes and edges, when
decisions happen, and how success or failure is handled.
It's like building a flowchart for agents, perfect for visual debugging.
Langraph is great for building complex workflows by integrating LLMs, tools, memory, and lang
chain functionalities, all in one place.
It's best suited for multi-step processes, document workflows, and decision trees.
Autogen works by running conversations between agents, and between agents and humans.
It's designed for intuitive collaboration, where each agent helps solve problems in real-time.
It excels in conversational interactions, clarifying goals, asking questions, and delegating work.
This makes it ideal for human-in-the-loop workflows, where users can step in to guide
or approve actions.
You can define agent personas and prototype how they work together.
You can even embed live code execution into conversations.
Autogen is especially useful in education, collaborative coding, and technical support
systems.
Pydantic AI brings structure to every step by combining language generation with type
validation.
It ensures that outputs follow clear schemas, reducing errors and ambiguity.
Perfect for APIs, structured data, and enterprise use, especially in fields where stability
matters.
Its modular design makes schema management simple.
From prototype to production, Pydantic AI delivers confidence and control.
Note, we will not cover Pydantic AI in this course, as it is relatively new.
BAI is flexible, letting you design the multi-agent system your use case needs.
From simple React agents to complex setups, you control agent behavior.
It integrates with tools such as Ollama, OpenAI, WatsonX.AI, and Grok.
Plug-in lang chain tools connect via the model context protocol or build your own.
BAI scales to production with memory, structured outputs, and state persistence.
Its emitters track workflows while telemetry and logging support debugging.
It's reliable, handling failures with clean exceptions.
For enterprise-grade systems, BAI offers power, precision, and control.
CruAI and Autogen offer the smoothest onboarding, ideal for rapid prototyping, education, or
lightweight systems that benefit from conversational flow and collaboration.
Langraph and Pydantic AI bring more complexity but offer greater control.
Langraph suits structured workflows, state management, and error recovery.
Pydantic AI ensures reliable, type-safe outputs, perfect for enterprise systems and APIs.
BAI focuses on enterprise-grade deployment, built for scalability and operational robustness.
Base frameworks are already in production.
CruAI powers content pipelines and automated reporting.
Langraph handles document workflows and customer service processes.
Autogen supports educational platforms and technical support systems.
Pydantic AI ensures reliable API services and data validation.
BAI manages enterprise automation.
Their applications are diverse and growing.
